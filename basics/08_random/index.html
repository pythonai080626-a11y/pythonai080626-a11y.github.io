Random & Modules 🎲

Every tool you have used so far — print, input, range — was built in. import unlocks the rest of Python: hundreds of ready-made toolboxes called modules. Open the first one, random, and your programs can finally roll dice, deal cards and draw a lottery.

dice.py
import random for _ in range(3): print(random.randint(1, 6))
1

import — Borrowing a Toolbox

A module is a file full of ready-written code that ships with Python but is not loaded until you ask for it. One line, at the top of your file, once:

import random

That single name is now the handle to everything inside:

random.randintwhole number in a range random.randomfloat 0.0 → 0.999… random.choicepick one item random.randrangepick from a range with a step random.sampleseveral, no repeats random.seedmake it repeatable
num = random.randint(1, 6) # module . function
⚠️ import random does not pour the names into your program — it creates the one name random. Calling randint(1, 6) without the dot is a NameError.

help() — The Manual, Inside Python

Not sure whether randint(1, 6) can actually return 6? Do not guess — ask:

import random help(random.randint)
Help on method randint in module random: randint(a, b) method of random.Random instance Return random integer in range [a, b], including both end points.
💡 "including both end points" — question answered, in the one place that can never be out of date. help() works on anything: help(print), help(str), help(random) for the whole module. In PyCharm, Ctrl+Q on a name shows the same text in a popup.
3

randint — Rolling Dice 🎲

random.randint(a, b) gives a whole number between a and b, both included.

x = random.randint(10, 20) # 10, 11, 12 ... 19 or 20 print(x)
← random.randint(1, 6), five times
⚠️ randint includes the top; range does not. random.randint(1, 6) can return 6, while range(1, 6) stops at 5. Two functions, two rules — this is the classic mix-up.

Many numbers at once — with a throwaway _:

# print 50 random numbers between 1 and 36 for _ in range(50): num = random.randint(1, 36) print(num, end=' ') print()
4

seed — The Same "Random" Every Run

Computers cannot really be random. They run a formula whose output looks random, and the formula needs a starting point — the seed. Python normally takes it from the clock, so every run differs. Set it yourself and the whole sequence repeats exactly:

random.seed(42) for _ in range(50): print(random.randint(1, 36), end=' ')
run #1 — seed 42
8 2 18 16 15 9 7 35 6 28 3 2 6 14 15 33 2 36 13 35 27 15 29 18 1 11 28 22 18 10 14 22 7 6 25 7 23 23 17 3 30 35 8 25 6 36 19 24 13 5
run #2 — seed 42
8 2 18 16 15 9 7 35 6 28 3 2 6 14 15 33 2 36 13 35 27 15 29 18 1 11 28 22 18 10 14 22 7 6 25 7 23 23 17 3 30 35 8 25 6 36 19 24 13 5
SituationSeed it?
a real game — dice, cards, lotteryno. Let the clock decide, so every game differs.
chasing a bug that only appears "sometimes"yes. Same numbers every run = a bug you can reproduce.
homework whose output must match the teacher'syes. Everyone gets identical output.
⚠️ Call random.seed() once, at the start. Inside a loop it restarts the sequence on every round — and you get the same number over and over.

random() and choice() — Floats and Non-Numbers

random() — a float 0.0 … 0.999…
f = random.random() print(f)
0.6394267984578837
choice() — one item from a group
for _ in range(10): print(random.choice([1, 3, 5, None, -2, 7, "hello"]), end=" ")
5 3 None 7 1 1 hello -2 1 5
🎯 random.random() takes no arguments and always spans the same range — 0.0 is possible, 1.0 never is. It is perfect for chances: if random.random() < 0.3: happens roughly 30% of the time.
💡 choice is the one that escapes numbers — words, None, anything you list in [ ]. Items can repeat between calls (1 came out three times above), and the square brackets make a list, the subject of a whole future lesson.
6

randrange() and sample() — Steps and No Repeats

randrange takes the same three numbers as range() and hands you one of its members:

# the members of range(3, 30 + 3, 3) → 3 6 9 12 15 18 21 24 27 30 # identical to random.choice([3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18, 21, 24, 27, 30]) print(random.randrange(3, 30 + 3, 3))
18
🧭 randrange follows the range rule — the stop is excluded. randint does not. Remember it by the name.

Call randint six times for a lottery and the same ball eventually comes out twice. sample is the function that cannot repeat itself:

# no repetition - 1 time each number print(random.sample(range(1, 10 + 1), 10))
[6, 3, 7, 10, 1, 8, 5, 2, 4, 9]
637101 85249
💡 All ten numbers came out, each exactly once, in a shuffled order — sample with k equal to the group size is a shuffle. Ask for fewer and you get a draw: random.sample(range(1, 37), 6) is a lottery ticket.
>>> random.sample(range(1, 5), 10) ValueError: Sample larger than population or is negative
⚠️ There are only 4 numbers in range(1, 5) — no way to hand out 10 different ones.
